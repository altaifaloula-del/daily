# New Arabic dictionary-word brand names for a Saudi cloud ERP: brainstorm and conflict vetting (Nov 2026)

Method: brainstormed about 30 words from the roots ج م ع، ض م م، ح و ي، ل م م، ك م ل، ش م ل، ك ن ف، ر ك ز، ك ن ز، ر ز م، ح ز م، ل ف ف، ق ط ب، ظ ل ل. Removed the excluded names and their close variants (Ashmal, Shamel, Kulli, Ihata and the others). Ran English and Arabic web searches on 25 words, using the word plus app, software, platform, منصة, تطبيق and شركة. Most hits were App Store listings. A search with no hits is evidence the name is free, not proof: I did not query SAIP's database, WIPO's Global Brand Database or the domain registries directly (see Gaps).

Brainstorm pool, all searched: قاطبة، لفيف، مكنز، مظلة، رزمة، كافّة، أكناف، كنانة، سِوار، رُكن، حزمة، مجمع، لُحمة، حصيلة، كنف، حاوي، ركيزة، لمّة، نواة، محور، جامع، عِقد، أجمع، وعاء.

## Q1. Which candidate words are free of software, SaaS, fintech or app use in KSA, the GCC and global software?

### Takeaway
None of the shortlist turned up a software, ERP or fintech product with the same name. The cleanest are **قاطبة Qatiba** and **مكنز Maknaz**, with no exact-name product found in English or Arabic. **لفيف Lafeef** and **مظلة Mazalla** come next: each clashes only with a small non-software app. Nine words were rejected because a Saudi or GCC software, fintech or government platform already uses the name or a near-identical one.

### Cited Findings

#### Kept shortlist (ranked best first)

**1. قاطبة: Qatiba (also Qatibah, Qaatiba)**
- Meaning: "all together, every one without exception" (جاؤوا قاطبةً, "they all came, to the last one"). It expresses totality directly but is not a word for software.
- Fit: the most direct "everything, nothing left out" word that is still free. It suits "every module of the business in one platform".
- Conflicts found: none. The English search for "Qatiba" app/software/platform found no product of that name; the results were only Qatar-related apps such as Lzhiba and QIB — [search results incl. Lzhiba](https://apps.apple.com/my/app/lzhiba/id1550117700). The Arabic search for "قاطبة" with تطبيق/منصة/شركة found no entity — [unrelated results e.g. Hawsabah](https://creatio.com/partners/hawsabah-hwsbt). The trademark/startup search found no "Qatibah" mark — [Justia results (none matching)](https://trademarks.justia.com/974/39/lafi-97439352.html).
- English pronunciation: KAA-ti-ba. The ق is often softened to K, and an English reader may see "Qatiba" as related to Qatar. In Arabic it should not be confused with كتيبة katība ("battalion"): the vowel length and the letter ق differ.
- Risk verdict: **LOW**. This is the best-vetted option.

**2. مكنز: Maknaz (also Makniz)**
- Meaning: from كنز ("treasure"), a place where valuables are stored. In modern Arabic it also means "thesaurus", a structured collection of every term.
- Fit: a "treasury and store of all your business data". It also connects to inventory (مخزون) and ledgers.
- Conflicts found: no exact match in English. Near names are Maknon Design (tile-trader software) and MaktApp (a Qatar ERP; different sound) — [Maknon Design](https://apps.apple.com/app/id1570586538), [MaktApp, Doha ERP](https://zoftwarehub.com/en-bh/products/maktapp/overview). No platform or app named "مكنز" turned up in Arabic — [search result page with unrelated items](https://khamsat.com/services/برنامج-مخازن).
- English pronunciation: MAK-naz. Easy to say and spell, with no negative English meaning.
- Risk verdict: **LOW**. MaktApp is a Gulf ERP, so its "Mak-" sound is a slight overlap; check SAIP class 9/42 for "Mak-" marks.

**3. لفيف: Lafeef (also Lafif)**
- Meaning: a crowd of different kinds gathered together (لفيف من الناس, "a mixed gathering"). From لفّ ("to wrap or bundle").
- Fit: "wraps up" sales, POS, stock and HR in one place.
- Conflicts found: an iOS app **"Lafif لفيف"** is a Saudi auto-parts store (developer Top Line, site lafif.com.sa) — [App Store: Lafif لفيف](https://apps.apple.com/app/id1499914529). The English search found no software company named Lafeef — [search results incl. Lifa App](https://www.crunchbase.com/organization/lifa-app-ltd). There is also a Riyadh bike-share app "Laffah لفّة", spelled differently — [Laffah](https://apps.apple.com/sa/app/laffah/id1512088971).
- English pronunciation: la-FEEF. Easy; "Lafeef" sounds a little playful in English.
- Risk verdict: **LOW–MEDIUM**. A KSA business already uses the same Arabic word, but for auto-parts retail, not software. The domain lafif.com.sa is taken, so use a spelling such as Lafeef plus a suffix.

**4. مظلة: Mazalla, Mazallah or Madhalla ("umbrella")**
- Meaning: umbrella or canopy. "Under one umbrella" (تحت مظلة واحدة) is a common Arabic phrase for consolidation.
- Fit: very strong metaphor for "all modules under one umbrella".
- Conflicts found: the Saudi English search found no "Mazalla" or "Madhalla" app — [results e.g. Mahally](https://apps.apple.com/app/id6443902847). The Arabic search found only a small "مظله ستور" store app run by an organization called مظلة; its country is not stated — [Arabic search results](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/-/id6470996069).
- English pronunciation: the letter ظ has no standard spelling (Mazalla, Madhalla, Mathalla, Muzalla), which is the main weakness. "Mazalla" may remind English speakers of "mozzarella". Many companies use the English word "Umbrella", but the Arabic brand avoids that.
- Risk verdict: **MEDIUM**, because of the spelling and because the word is common. Not descriptive of software, so it should be registrable.

**5. رزمة: Rizma or Ruzma ("bundle, packet")**
- Meaning: a bundle or packet of things tied together (رزمة أوراق, "a bundle of papers").
- Fit: "the full bundle".
- Conflicts found: no exact match. Near names are **Rozm**, an e-commerce marketplace app (Rozm Inc.), and **Rizzma**, a charisma and social-skills app — [Rozm](https://apps.apple.com/lb/app/rozm/id6608961067), [Rizzma](https://apps.apple.com/lb/app/rizzma/id6738768179). No Saudi platform called "رزمة" turned up — [Arabic search results](https://www.wafeq.com/ar/).
- English pronunciation: RIZ-ma. It is close to the slang "rizz", which some readers may find trivial, and the Arabic vowel is unclear (rizma or ruzma).
- Risk verdict: **MEDIUM**.

**6. كافّة: Kaffah or Kaffa ("all, the whole")**
- Meaning: "all, entirely" (للناس كافّة, "for all people"). A totality word with Quranic resonance.
- Fit: direct "everything".
- Conflicts found: no Saudi software platform named Kaffah or Kaffa. The results were coffee apps: COFE, Qavashop كافا شوب, Kiffa Roaster — [COFE](https://apps.apple.com/kw/app/cofe/id1339438881), [Qavashop](https://apps.apple.com/pl/app/qavashop-%D9%83%D8%A7%D9%81%D8%A7-%D8%B4%D9%88%D8%A8/id1469195910).
- English pronunciation: KAF-fa. It strongly suggests coffee, and "Kaffa" is the Ethiopian region that gave coffee its name (general knowledge, not sourced here). Search results will be crowded with coffee businesses.
- Risk verdict: **MEDIUM**. Not taken, but weak for SEO and distinctiveness.

**7. أكناف: Aknaf (plural of كَنَف, "sides, shelter, protective wing")**
- Meaning: في أكنافه means "under his care and protection".
- Fit: "your business, embraced and sheltered".
- Conflicts found: a small free iOS utility app **"Aknaf"** (© Aknaf - اكناف, developer Islam Abou Elsaoud, Egyptian store) for reporting problems — [App Store: Aknaf](https://apps.apple.com/eg/app/aknaf/id6475650401). The singular **كنف Kanaf** is taken in KSA (see Rejected).
- English pronunciation: AK-naf. Easy.
- Risk verdict: **MEDIUM**. A small app shares the name, and there is a family resemblance to the Kanaf platform built for Takamol.

**8. كنانة: Kenana or Kinana ("a quiver that holds all the arrows")**
- Meaning: a quiver. Egypt is called "كنانة الله", and the word is also a tribal name.
- Fit: "a quiver holding every tool you need".
- Conflicts found: no "Kenana" software in KSA. The Saudi e-commerce platform **Kanaa** (launched April 2026, backed by Al Nahla Group) sounds similar — [Zawya: Kanaa launch](https://www.zawya.com/en/press-release/companies-news/kanaa-launches-in-saudi-arabia-aiming-to-redefine-local-e-commerce-experience-pa7p8fja), [Sharikat Mubasher](https://en.sharikatmubasher.com/media-hub/news/21527093/kanaa-enters-saudi-ecommerce-market-with-ai-driven-platform?lang=en). From general knowledge, not checked in this pass: Kenana Sugar Company (Sudan) and the former Arabic site-hosting platform "Kenana Online".
- English pronunciation: ke-NAA-na. Easy.
- Risk verdict: **MEDIUM**. Kanaa is a sound-alike in Saudi e-commerce.

**9. سِوار: Siwar ("bracelet, encircling band")**
- Meaning: a bracelet. The image is "encircling" or "going all the way round".
- Fit: a weaker fit, "a circle around your whole business".
- Conflicts found: **Siwar Delivery**, a logistics app (Rubik Smart Solutions) — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/-/id6466994652). **Assiwar** is a charity's support-chat app — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/kh/app/assiwar/id6739460150). **Sewar** is a food company's app — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/cn/app/sewar/id6447311938).
- English pronunciation: si-WAAR. It could be misread as "sewer", which is a real problem in English.
- Risk verdict: **MEDIUM–HIGH**.

**10. رُكن: Rukn ("pillar, corner, cornerstone")**
- Meaning: a foundational pillar (أركان الإسلام, "the pillars of Islam").
- Fit: foundation, "the cornerstone of your business".
- Conflicts found: **Rukn**, an Islamic companion app developed in Singapore — [rukn-one](https://rukn-one.pages.dev/about). **Rukn ركن**, a lifestyle and prayer app — [mwm.ai](https://mwm.ai/apps/rukn/6760690395). **Rukn**, a workspace and restaurant in Kuwait — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/lb/app/rukn/id6762558991). No ERP found.
- English pronunciation: "Rukn" has no vowel between k and n, which is awkward for English speakers, and the word carries religious connotations.
- Risk verdict: **MEDIUM–HIGH**. Many apps already share the name, and Arkan (أركان) is a common company name.

### Inferences
- Classical, rarer totality words (قاطبة, لفيف, مكنز) are still free. Common, positive words such as لمّة, نواة, ركيزة, كنف and محور are already taken by Saudi startups.
- **Qatiba** and **Maknaz** are the strongest new options. Qatiba says "all of it" most directly; Maknaz adds a "treasury of your data" angle that suits accounting and inventory.

### Gaps
- I did not query the SAIP trademark search, WIPO Global Brand Database, Saudi CR (Wathq/MC), .sa/.com domains or Google Play directly for each kept name. The owner should run SAIP classes 9, 35 and 42 searches for Qatiba, Maknaz, Lafeef and Mazalla before committing.
- LinkedIn and Crunchbase were checked only through general web search, not native search.
- "Kenana Online" and Kenana Sugar come from my own knowledge and were not verified in this pass.

## Q2. Which words are too generic or descriptive to register as a trademark with SAIP?

### Takeaway
The highest descriptive risk is with words that name the product or its function in Arabic: **حزمة** ("software package"), **مجمع** ("complex"), **جامع**, **عقد** ("contract") and **كافّة** (a general quantifier). **قاطبة, مكنز, لفيف, مظلة, أكناف and كنانة** are suggestive or arbitrary for ERP software and should be registrable.

### Cited Findings
- I found no SAIP rulings on these specific words in this pass. The brief states that SAIP rejects purely descriptive words; I did not verify a source for that here.
- حزمة is the ordinary Arabic term for a software bundle or package; this is a lexical judgment, and no other product uses it as an exact-name brand — [Hazmi near-match](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/-/id6452684779).

### Inferences
- قاطبة ("all, without exception") is a laudatory or quantifying adverb but does not describe ERP goods or services, so it is likely registrable. The same applies to مكنز (treasury/thesaurus) and لفيف (gathered crowd), which are metaphorical.
- مظلة ("umbrella") is a common noun but arbitrary for software, so it is likely registrable. Its ordinary use as "umbrella organisation" may weaken how strongly it can be enforced.
- كافّة is a function word ("all"). Registering it alone may be harder, and a stylised logo or compound such as "Kaffah ERP" would help.

### Gaps
- No access to SAIP's examination guidelines or past refusals for single Arabic common words. A local IP agent should confirm.

## Q3. Which have English pronunciation or meaning problems?

### Takeaway
**Siwar** (reads as "sewer"), **Rizma** ("rizz" slang), **Kaffah** (reads as coffee), **Mazalla** (the ظ has no fixed spelling; may suggest "mozzarella") and **Rukn** (consonant cluster) have English issues. **Maknaz, Lafeef, Aknaf, Kenana and Qatiba** are mostly clean, though Qatiba's Q may be read as "Qatar".

### Cited Findings
- Coffee businesses dominate search results for Kaffa/Kaffah: COFE, Qavashop, Kiffa Roaster — [COFE](https://apps.apple.com/kw/app/cofe/id1339438881), [Kiffa Roaster](https://apps.apple.com/cz/app/kiffa-roaster/id1471319951).
- "Rizzma" is already an app built on "rizz" slang for charisma — [Rizzma](https://apps.apple.com/lb/app/rizzma/id6738768179).
- "Qatiba" queries return mostly Qatar-related apps, which shows the Qatar association — [Qatar search results](https://apps.apple.com/mt/app/qatar-pay/id1556624460).

### Inferences
- Suggested English spellings: **Qatiba** (not "Katiba"), **Maknaz**, **Lafeef** (the double "ee" shows the long vowel and avoids the taken "Lafif"), **Mazalla** (simplest of the ظ spellings).
- Pronunciation and readability notes for Siwar, Rukn and Mazalla are my linguistic judgment, not sourced.

### Gaps
- No native English-speaker testing or survey data. These are analyst judgments.
