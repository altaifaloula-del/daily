# Coined / brandable Arabic-rooted names for a Saudi cloud ERP (vetting as of Nov 2026)

Method: 22 coined or semi-coined candidates were built from short Arabic roots (ج م ع، ح ز م، ل ف ف، و س ق، ق ط ب، ح و ط، د م ج، ر ز م، ج م ل، ك ف ي، ل م م، ح و ي، ك ن ش، ن ظ م). Each was web-searched in English (and the main ones in Arabic) for software, fintech or app use. App Store and Play listings were the most useful signal. The excluded list in the brief was avoided. Limits: domain fetches were blocked by the sandbox network (DNS failure for every domain tried), the search engine is US-centric and weak for Arabic, and WIPO/SAIP trademark databases were not queried directly. "No conflict found" therefore means none in web, App Store or Play results. It does not mean the trademark is clear.

## Q1. Which coined names have no existing software/fintech/app usage (KSA/GCC and globally)?

### Takeaway
Nine candidates returned no software or fintech product with the same name. The cleanest are **Qatba (قطبة)**, **Wasaq (وَسَق)** and **Lafeef (لفيف)**. Next come **Hazma (حزمة)**, **Ajmaa (أجمع)**, **Mujmal (مُجمَل)**, **Ihata (إحاطة)**, **Damj (دمج)** and **Rizma (رِزمة)**, each with a near-name or language caveat. Thirteen candidates were rejected because they collide with live apps or products, several of them Saudi accounting or fintech products.

### Cited Findings

**Kept candidates (no same-name software/fintech found)**

1. **Qatba / قطبة**. Formed from قاطبة ("all, without exception", as in جاؤوا قاطبةً) and shortened to two syllables, so it evokes "everything, entirely". Conflicts: none found. An English search returned only Qatar-related apps ([search result list incl. Qatar Airways app](https://apps.apple.com/gb/app/qatar-airways/id581264644)). A company search also returned nothing named Qatba ([Qatar Nukhba companies list, no match](https://qe.com.qa/en/web/nukhba/companies)). A combined trademark-style query for Qatba, Wasaq and Lafeef returned no records ([UK IPO TM journal hit was unrelated](https://www.ipo.gov.uk/t-tmj/tm-journals/2026-032/UK00004411945.html)). Pronunciation: "KAT-ba", easy to say, though English speakers may soften ق to K. Risks: search engines auto-correct it to "Qatar". In Arabic قُطْبة also reads as "a stitch" and قَطَبَ means "to frown" (from general Arabic knowledge, not sourced). Verdict: **LOW risk**.
2. **Wasaq / وَسَق**. A Quranic root: «وَاللَّيْلِ وَمَا وَسَقَ» means "and what it gathers and envelops". وَسْق is also a classical unit of measure (a camel-load), so the name evokes "gathers everything" plus "measure", which suits accounting. Conflicts: none named Wasaq or Wasq. The only near-names are Wasel Apps (Jordan, B2B client interaction) ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/fr/app/wasel-apps/id1469013334)), Waasl (social planning) ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/pe/app/waasl/id6475218411)) and Wasapi ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/be/app/wasapi/id6443781383)). An Arabic search for "وسق" found no app or platform ([search, no match](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/-/id1498024810)). Pronunciation: "wa-SAQ". The final ق is hard in English and will be said "wasak". Other languages: no known negative meaning. Verdict: **LOW risk**. The Wasel/Waasl similarity is weak and in other classes.
3. **Lafeef / لفيف**. Means "an assembled mixed group" (Quran 17:104 «جِئْنَا بِكُمْ لَفِيفًا») and is also a grammar term for a doubly-weak root. It evokes "everything wrapped together" (لفّ = to wrap). Conflicts: none named Lafeef or Lafif. Near-names are LÁFRE (Malaysian personal-care app) ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/my/app/l%C3%A1fre/id1571673632)) and Lafi لافي, a food-delivery app ([mwm.ai](https://mwm.ai/apps/lafi/6505079679)). A brand search found no Lafeef brand ([search, closest Lattafa "Afeef"](https://www.notino.co.uk/lattafa/afeef/)). Pronunciation: "la-FEEF", clear in both scripts. Risk: Lafi لافي is phonetically close in KSA but is food delivery, not software. Verdict: **LOW risk**.
4. **Hazma / حزمة**. Means "bundle or package", which evokes "all modules in one package". Conflicts: no Hazma product. Near-name **Hazmi**, an iOS B2B app for tracking physical payment transactions (CODESAP Technologies LLP), is in a fintech-adjacent class ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/-/id6452684779)). Azma is an asthma app built for AstraZeneca KSA ([websquids](https://websquids.com/our-work/azma-mobile-app-for-tracking-and-managing-asthma-symptoms)). A Saudi-software search found nothing named Hazma ([Blazma closest](https://blazma.com/about?lang=en)). English: close to "hazmat" and a transposition of the name Hamza. Verdict: **LOW–MEDIUM risk**, because Hazmi is a payments-related near-name.
5. **Ajmaa / أجمع**. Means "all together, entire" (أجمعون). Conflicts: none found in a Saudi app/platform search ([results incl. Ajzal Benaa, unrelated](https://apps.apple.com/kw/app/ajzal-benaa/id1633191125)). Latin spelling is awkward (Ajmaa / Ajma / Ajmaʿ) and may be confused with Ajman (UAE). Verdict: **LOW–MEDIUM risk**. The name is clean, but the Latin form is weak.
6. **Mujmal / مُجمَل**. Means "total, sum" (المجمل = grand total), which fits accounting. Conflicts: none found ([search returned only unrelated accounting apps such as Rojmel](https://apps.apple.com/app/id1051302195)). Caveats: it is a dictionary word, so it is less registrable. In uṣūl al-fiqh, المُجمَل also means "ambiguous, needing clarification" (the opposite of المبيَّن), an unhelpful nuance for an accounting product (general knowledge, not sourced). Verdict: **MEDIUM risk** (registrability and meaning).
7. **Ihata / إحاطة**. Means "encompassing, full awareness" (أحاط بكل شيء علمًا). Conflicts: none found in English searches ([Saudi software search, no match](https://lean.sa/en/products/tarmeez)) or company searches ([unrelated Japanese results](https://www.cbinsights.com/compare/hata-vs-strophe)). Pronunciation problem: English readers may say "I-HATE-a", and "Ehata" is a safer Latin spelling. In Arabic, إحاطة is also used for a "briefing", as in a UN briefing. Verdict: **MEDIUM risk** (English pronunciation).
8. **Damj / دمج**. Means "merge, integration". Conflicts: none found ([Saudi tech search, no match](https://www.arabnews.pk/node/2294931); [app search returned only generic "merge apps" docs](https://docs.appmysite.com/app/merge-apps/combine-multiple-apps)). The Latin cluster "-mj" is hard to say and close to English "damn". A variant such as "Damjo" or "Dameej" would read more easily. Verdict: **MEDIUM risk** (phonetics).
9. **Rizma / رِزمة**. Means "bundle, ream" and sounds like "charisma". Conflicts: no software or fintech use. Rizzma is a consumer charisma-coaching app ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/lb/app/rizzma/id6738768179)) and RHIZA is BDO's risk-management software ([BDO](https://www.bdo.co.uk/en-gb/microsites/rhiza-enterprise-risk-management-software)). English risk: "rizz" is Gen-Z slang for flirting charm, which may read as unserious for an ERP. Verdict: **MEDIUM risk**.

**Rejected candidates (reason and URL)**
- **Jumla / جملة** ("total"): Jomla is a GCC e-commerce app (JOMLA E-COMMERCE FZC) ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/app/id1493216981)). There is also a "Jumla | جملة" app ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id1638838976)), the Saudi wholesale platform Jomlah ([Webkul](https://marketplace.webkul.com/success-stories/jomlah/)) and Jumlah Qareeb, a Riyadh delivery app ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id1515738819)).
- **Sijla / سِجلّة** (ledger): too close to **Sajlha**, a Saudi cloud accounting and ZATCA e-invoicing product with POS ([Capterra](https://www.capterra.com/p/10037099/Sajlha/)). This is a direct competitor conflict.
- **Hasbah / حسبة**: "الحسبة برو: محاسبة ومشاريع" is an accounting app on Google Play ([Play developer Hsba](https://play.google.com/store/apps/developer?id=Hsba)). Hesabat and Hesaab are also nearby ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/za/app/hesabat/id1444676471); [Salla Hesaab](https://help.salla.sa/en/article/hesaab-accounting-app/cpkb583cgcczpmm5073i3qlj)).
- **Kunnash / كنّاش** (notebook-ledger): **Konnash** is a ledger and cash-book app with more than 5M installs ([liners.com](https://liners.com/konnash)).
- **Nazma / نظمة**: Nazma Software Solution (India) sells billing software ([IndiaMART](https://m.indiamart.com/nazma-software-solution)), and the Nazam marketplace app runs in the UAE ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/ae/app/nazam/id6443635892)).
- **Jamio / جامِيو**: **jamio openwork** is a no-code BPM platform (jamio.com) ([Krowdbase](https://www.krowdbase.com/software/jamio-openwork)). A Jamio music app also exists ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/be/app/jamio/id6746352092)).
- **Kullo / كُلّو** ("all of it", Gulf colloquial): Kullo is an existing encrypted messenger with a GitHub protocol library ([GitHub](https://github.com/kullo/client-libkullo); [Softpedia listing 2024](https://mac.softpedia.com/downloadTag/encrypted%20chat)).
- **Kollah / Kulluh / كُلّه**: no direct software hit ([search](https://clutch.co/go-to-review/kulkul-technology/58244)). It is close to the Saudi government app "Kollona Amn" ([CTV](https://ctvnews.ca/sci-tech/saudi-snitching-app-kollona-amn-turns-citizens-into-social-media-police-1.6053220)), and the Latin spelling is unstable. Rejected as weak.
- **Lumma / لُمّة**: "Lumma Stealer" (LummaC2) is a notorious infostealer malware sold as a service since 2022 ([MITRE ATT&CK S1213](https://attack.mitre.org/software/S1213); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lumma_Stealer)). That is fatal for a finance SaaS. Side note for the excluded list: Wikipedia and the search summary attribute the malware's author to an actor called "Shamel" ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lumma_Stealer)). This is another reason to avoid Shamel.
- **Lamma / لمّة**: several Saudi apps use the name: Lamma | لمة food app ([App Store SA](https://apps.apple.com/sa/app/id1542017162)), a Lamma events app ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/lamma-app/id1605119039)) and Lamma Waleema ([mwm.ai](https://mwm.ai/apps/lamma-waleema/6593679665)). Lamaa is also a Riyadh fintech holding company ([CB Insights](https://www.cbinsights.com/company/lamaa)).
- **Takfi / تكفي** ("it suffices"): TKFA is a Jeddah delivery app ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/dk/app/tkfa/id1619970995)). It is also phonetically close to "Takfir" ([Wikipedia](https://EN.wikipedia.com/wiki/Takfir)).
- **Hawya / Hiwa / حاوية-حِواء** ("container"): Hawee is a Kuwaiti service app ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/jp/app/hawee-app/id1447393451?l=en-US)) and Hawi | هاوي is in the Saudi App Store ([App Store SA](https://apps.apple.com/sa/app/hawi-%D9%87%D8%A7%D9%88%D9%8A/id1594176538)). Hiwa is also a common Kurdish given name.
- **Kaffa / كافّة** ("all"): Kaffa is the Ethiopian coffee region and a coffee-roastery brand ([Odoo customer page](https://www.odoo.com/ro_RO/customers/kaffa-roastery-4129519)). It is close to **Kafinea**, a cloud ERP ([G2](https://www.g2.com/products/kafinea/reviews)).
- **Tamam / تمام** ("complete"): Tamam is Zain KSA's SAMA-licensed micro-finance fintech app ([Zain KSA](https://sa.zain.com/about/zain-ksas-fintech-arm-tamam-granted-first-consumer-micro-financing-license-in-kingdom/)).

### Inferences
- Names built from rare, classical or Quranic gathering words (قاطبة، وسق، لفيف) are the least used commercially. Common "total" or "bundle" words (جملة، حسبة، كناش، لمة) are already taken by apps, often accounting or fintech ones in the region.
- The Saudi accounting SaaS space is crowded with ledger and accounting words (Sajlha, Hesaab, Hesabat, Konnash, Daftra, Qoyod, Wafeq). Any name built on سجل، حساب، دفتر is likely to collide.
- Recommended shortlist order: Qatba > Wasaq > Lafeef > Hazma > Ajmaa, with Ihata (spelled Ehata), Damj, Mujmal and Rizma as backups.

### Gaps
- No direct check was made in the WIPO Global Brand Database, the SAIP (Saudi IP Authority) register or the GCC trademark registers. These must be checked before filing, especially for Qatba, Wasaq and Lafeef in Nice classes 9, 35, 36 and 42.
- LinkedIn, Crunchbase and Magnitt were not searched directly. "No conflict" rests on general web, App Store and Play results.

## Q2. Which are easy to say and spell in Arabic and English and stay meaningful to Arabic speakers?

### Takeaway
**Lafeef**, **Qatba** and **Hazma** are the easiest in both scripts. **Wasaq** has the strongest meaning (gather plus measure, Quranic) but its final Q sound is hard for English speakers. **Ihata** ("I hate-a"), **Damj** ("damn") and **Rizma** ("rizz") carry English pronunciation or slang risks.

### Cited Findings
- Search engines silently "correct" Qatba to Qatar, which shows a findability risk until the brand builds SEO ([search results returned only Qatar apps](https://apps.apple.com/gb/app/qatar-airways/id581264644)).
- Rizzma shows that "Rizma" will be read in English through the "rizz/charisma" slang lens ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/lb/app/rizzma/id6738768179)).
- Lafeef is phonetically near the Saudi food app Lafi لافي ([mwm.ai](https://mwm.ai/apps/lafi/6505079679)). Wasaq is near Wasel and Waasl ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/pe/app/waasl/id6475218411)).

### Inferences
- Meaning to Arabic speakers: Qatba (قاطبة, "all") and Lafeef (لفيف, "assembled group") are immediately understood as "everyone, everything together". Wasaq is understood by Quran-literate users and is less obvious to others. Hazma and Damj are instantly clear (bundle, merge). Mujmal reads as "total" in accounting but can mean "vague" in fiqh.
- Suggested Latin spellings: Qatba (or Katba for English ease), Wasaq, Lafeef, Hazma, Ehata (not Ihata), Ajma.

### Gaps
- No native-speaker or consumer pronunciation testing was done. Negative meanings in other languages (Urdu, Turkish, Malay, Persian) were checked only from general knowledge, and no sourced negatives were found for the kept names.

## Q3. Are there signs the matching .com / .sa domains are in use?

### Takeaway
This could not be determined. Direct fetches of qatba.com, wasaq.com, lafeef.com, hazma.com, ihata.com, ajmaa.com, damj.com and rizma.com all failed with DNS "ENOTFOUND" inside the research sandbox, which restricts outbound hosts. The failure is an environment limit, not evidence that the domains are free.

### Cited Findings
- None of the kept names' .com or .sa domains appeared as active sites in any search result. By contrast, rejected names showed live domains: jamio.com ([Krowdbase](https://www.krowdbase.com/software/jamio-openwork)), tkfa.com ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/dk/app/tkfa/id1619970995)) and tamam.life ([Zain KSA](https://sa.zain.com/about/zain-ksas-fintech-arm-tamam-granted-first-consumer-micro-financing-license-in-kingdom/)).

### Inferences
- Absence from search results weakly suggests that the kept names' .com domains are parked or unused rather than run by an operating business. Short five-letter .coms (qatba.com, hazma.com, rizma.com) are likely registered or parked. Less common forms (lafeef.com, wasaq.com, ehata.com) have a better chance of being free or cheap. This is unverified.

### Gaps
- WHOIS or registrar lookups for each .com and .sa (via nic.sa) were not possible because network access was blocked. These must be run manually, along with checks of handles on X, Instagram and LinkedIn.
